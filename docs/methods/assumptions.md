# Assumptions and interpretation

## Required assumptions

1. Relevance: selected variants predict the exposure. PlantMR uses exposure P values and an F-statistic threshold as diagnostics, not as proof of relevance.
2. Independence: instruments are not associated with unmeasured exposure–outcome confounders. Population structure, kinship, geography, breeding history and environment can violate this assumption in plant panels.
3. Exclusion restriction: instruments affect the outcome only through the exposure. Horizontal pleiotropy can violate this assumption; MR-Egger and heterogeneity diagnostics are sensitivity analyses, not repairs.
4. Harmonized effects: exposure and outcome effects refer to the same effect allele and comparable genome/trait definitions.

## Plant-specific cautions

- A reference assembly mismatch can make an apparently matching SNP a different locus.
- A human LD panel must not be silently used for a plant population.
- Polyploid dosage, homoeolog identity, PAV and SV are not interchangeable with ordinary biallelic SNP dosage.
- Environment-specific effects should not be averaged when the biological question is stress response or adaptation.
- MR estimates genetic perturbation effects under its assumptions; they do not automatically equal the effect of a short-term agronomic treatment.

## Evidence language

Use “MR-supported candidate” or “MR-prioritized relationship” for statistical output. Reserve “causal gene” or “validated mechanism” for results supported by independent replication and functional evidence.
